Moving guide

Moving with kids, without the meltdowns.

Moves are big for children. Tell them early, give them small choices, keep routines steady and set up their room first—then the new place starts to feel like home.

Before the move

  • Tell them early and calmly. Acknowledge the change, and stress what stays the same—family, routines, favorite things.
  • Visit the new neighborhood if you can: parks, the library, the new school.
  • Let them choose small things, like what goes in their own essentials bag or a color for their new room.
  • Make a memory of the old home—photos of their room, a goodbye walk.
  • Request school and medical records early and contact the new school before the first day.

On moving day

  • Keep meals, naps and bedtime as normal as you can.
  • Arrange a sitter or a quiet, safe space away from the crew and the open doors.
  • Pack a kids’ bag: favorite toy, blanket, snacks, a tablet or book, a change of clothes.
  • Keep little ones away from ramps, stairs and the truck.

After the move

  • Set up their room first, with familiar bedding and toys.
  • Explore the new neighborhood together in the first week.
  • Expect a few rough days—and keep talking about what they miss and what they like.

Kids’ moving bag

Pack one for each child.

  • Favorite toy or blanket
  • Snacks and a water bottle
  • A book or tablet
  • Change of clothes
  • Night light

Common questions

Moving with kids questions

How do I tell my kids we’re moving?+

Tell them early, calmly and when you have their full attention. Acknowledge the change and focus on what stays the same.

Where should kids be on moving day?+

With a sitter or in a quiet, safe space away from the crew, open doors and the truck.

What should I unpack first for my kids?+

Their bedroom. Familiar bedding and toys help the new house feel like home on the first night.
