Packing that survives the truck

How to pack like the pros

Whether our crew packs everything or you pack a few rooms yourself, these habits prevent most moving-day damage.

  • Heavy items in small boxes. Books, tools and canned goods go in small boxes; bedding and lampshades in large ones. A box should lift comfortably.
  • No rattles, no bulges. Fill gaps with crumpled paper so nothing shifts, and tape both the top and bottom seams.
  • Plates on edge. Wrap dishes one at a time and stand plates on edge, like records. Mark the box FRAGILE and THIS SIDE UP.
  • Label two sides and the top. Write the destination room and a short list of contents so boxes land in the right place without being opened.
  • Pack an open-first box. Sheets, towels, toiletries, chargers, medication, a few tools, paper towels and snacks for the first night.
  • Leave hazardous items out. Propane, gasoline, paint, aerosols and pool chemicals generally can’t ride on a moving truck. Use them up or dispose of them safely.

Common questions

Can I pack some rooms myself and have you pack the rest?+

Yes. Partial packing is one of the options—for example, the crew can handle the kitchen and fragile rooms while you pack the rest.

When should I start packing?+

Start with rooms and items you use least, about two weeks out, and leave the kitchen and daily essentials for the last few days.

What can’t go on the moving truck?+

Hazardous and flammable items such as propane tanks, gasoline, paint, aerosols and pool chemicals. Keep medication, documents and valuables with you.
