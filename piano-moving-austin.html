Piano care

Before and after your piano moves

Pianos are heavy, top-heavy and sensitive to temperature and humidity. A little preparation protects the instrument and the house.

  • Clear the top and close it up. Remove photos and decor, close the lid and lock the fallboard if it has a lock.
  • Measure the path. Doorways, stair turns, landings and porch steps at both homes. Photos of the route help us plan.
  • Know the type. Spinet, console, upright, baby grand or grand. Grands are usually moved on their side on a padded piano board with the legs and pedal lyre removed.
  • Choose the new spot. An interior wall away from windows, air vents and fireplaces keeps temperature and humidity steadier.
  • Wait before tuning. Let the piano settle into its new room first. Piano technicians commonly suggest waiting a few weeks; never tune it the day it arrives.

Common questions

When should I tune my piano after moving?+

Give it time to adjust to the new room’s temperature and humidity—piano technicians commonly suggest two to four weeks—then schedule the tuning.

Do you move grand pianos?+

Yes. Uprights, baby grands and larger instruments each need different preparation, so tell us the type, size, stairs and turns at both locations.

What should I do before the crew arrives?+

Clear the top of the piano, close the lid, measure doorways and turns, and make sure the path is clear at both homes.
